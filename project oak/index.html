Vision · 4 of 15
What is missing

What's missing is what holds it all together.

The experience is fragmented and inconsistent. Patterns that look and behave differently from one module to the next. Interactions that work one way here and another way there. The more we scale, the more we add, the harder it gets to hold the experience together.

Home — launcher grid, distinct visual language from every other module.

People — two separate search bars on the same screen. Different nav, different grid.

Giving — billing error competing with data. Inconsistent cards, different information density.

Vision · 5 of 15
The missing piece

A platform that feels like a puzzle of disconnected pieces.

The features don't speak to each other either. Flows that start in one module and break apart when they cross into another. No threading line, no end to end thinking, no workflow that follows the actual shape of the work being done.

This isn't a feature problem. It isn't a visual problem. It's the absence of the layer that makes everything feel intentional, reliable, and designed for the human using it and their mission.

Vision · 6 of 15
What changes

What changes when product experience is treated as a strategic function.

When experience design sits at the start of every product decision, the product gets built around the people using it. Their needs become the brief. Their day becomes the constraint. The work of every other discipline gets sharper because it has a clear answer to the question of who it is for.

The function exists to hold the human at the center of decisions before those decisions get made. Not after, when the work is to translate someone else's logic into a usable surface. Before, when there is still time to make the product about the person it is for.

That is the function this initiative needs. That is what is missing today.

Vision · 7 of 15
The problem with how it works today

Good experience needs to be inevitable, not heroic.

Right now, every attempt at consistency, intuitiveness, end to end seamlessness is fighting against the way the work flows. Every push toward coherence runs into friction, bottlenecks, and the slow weight of misalignment. Patterns get reinvented module by module instead of shared. Handoffs are heavy and lossy.

Vision · 8 of 15
Where it shows

Where the product feels disconnected is a mirror of where the weight sits in the organisation.

Business, sales, engineering have always been at the strategic table. The discipline that holds the human at the center of decisions has not had the same seat, the same resourcing, the same partnership from the start.

A product built by two of the three legs of a real product organisation will always feel like one. That is what we are looking at.

Vision · 9 of 15

Vision · 10 of 15
The answer

So what makes product excellence inevitable.

A design system that holds the experience together. A shared foundation that turns consistency and coherence from the exception into the default.

And experience design at the strategic level. A partner in product decisions from the start, not a polish layer at the end.

When the design system and the experience design function are both properly resourced and given strategic weight, shipping a good experience stops being a battle and becomes the natural outcome of how the work gets done. That's what makes scaling at speed possible. That's what makes outrunning the competition possible.

Project Oak

The plan.

How NewCo becomes the platform that redefines mission-driven software. Sequenced, resourced, and measurable.

Project Oak  ·  Prepared by Jo Lopez Lluque

Plan · 1 of 17
The shape of the year

Two visible releases within the first year.

The first proves the model works. The second, landing alongside the rebrand already in motion, becomes the flagship moment when NewCo stops being one of the lot and becomes best in class.

Plan · 2 of 17
The shape of the work

Four phases. Measurement throughout.

Plan · 3 of 17
Phase 1 · Months 1–3

Foundation.

The infrastructure that makes everything else possible.

What gets done

Pipeline and contribution model documented and aligned with engineering. Token architecture cleaned up and governed: color, layout, motion. Figma upgraded for branching. Local versus global component model defined. Component acceleration workflow set up with Radix, tokens, and AI assistance. Baseline measurement framework defined.

Who does it

Director of Product Experience leading. Senior Product Designer (Design Systems) hired and ramped. Dedicated engineer onboarded. Project manager holding process.

Deliverable

A working, governed Pathway foundation ready to scale. A baseline to measure against. A team in place and operational.

Success looks like

The pipeline ships from Figma to production cleanly. Contribution model documented and followed. Baseline captured for navigation, search, and one core flow.

Plan · 4 of 17
Phase 2 · Months 4–7

Build.

The component library and the brand integration.

What gets done

Tables, search, filters, tabbed navigation, page layouts, form and workflow components, dropdowns, selects, popovers, modals, the full input set. Module and org switcher revamp. Amplify Home reworked for every module. Amplify Intelligence refresh. New brand theme added as a parallel token branch and applied across components. Usability testing on the highest impact components.

Who does it

The Phase 1 team, fully operational. Design system designer leading component work. Engineering shipping. Director negotiating priorities. Project manager holding roadmap. Coordination with marketing on brand integration.

Deliverable

A complete v1 component library with the new brand applied, tested, and ready for production launch.

Success looks like

Every new feature being built uses Pathway. Component reuse rate climbing. Implementation accuracy improving. Brand integration QA tested and ready.

Plan · 5 of 17
Phase 3 · Month 8

Launch.

Pathway and the rebrand ship together.

What gets done

Production deployment of Pathway across the product. Rebrand applied simultaneously. Coordinated launch with marketing. Metrics captured against baseline. The before and after story documented.

Deliverable

The flagship moment. A measurably better product. The story for the CEO and the market.

Success looks like

Capterra and G2 sentiment and review volume shifting. Experience-related support tickets dropping measurably. NPS moving against baseline. The launch read externally as a genuine product transformation, not a coat of paint.

Plan · 6 of 17
Phase 4 · Months 9+

Deepen.

The deeper transformation.

What gets done

Core flows and organisms redesigned end to end. Account settings, permissions, profiles, user management, sign in, empty states, error handling, form and workflow systems. The complex, high impact areas where the product has always struggled most.

Who does it

The full team, with additional dedicated designers brought in as the work expands, backed by launch data.

Deliverable

Continuous improvement to the deepest, most-used flows. Polish becomes real coherence.

Success looks like

Task completion climbing. Time on task dropping. Net revenue retention climbing as customers adopt more of the platform.

Plan · 7 of 17
Timing

Why ship them together.

The rebrand is already in motion. The recommendation is that it ships alongside Pathway, not before it.

Rebrand first, and it's a coat of paint over a product still fragmented underneath. The promise breaks the moment users start clicking.

Pathway first without the brand, and the improvement is real but quiet. The story stays internal, invisible to the market.

Together, they are one moment. A new look, a new feel, a new way the product works, all at once.

Resourcing

What this requires.

Director
A Director of Product Experience to own the vision, lead the platform tribe, and represent product experience at every strategic decision.
Design system
A lead designer dedicated fully to the design system.
Backfill
A designer covering Protections and Accounting so existing work continues uninterrupted.
PM
A project manager holding process, roadmap, and dependencies across all phases.
Engineering
One dedicated engineer for Phase 1 foundation work, with front-end engineering support scaling up for the Phase 2 component build.
Analytics
Existing analytics resources tapped for baseline measurement in Phase 1, with dedicated product analytics named as a future conversation. Getting data needs to be easier — this is a conversation worth starting now.
Plan · 10 of 17
Structure

One platform tribe. Two squads.

The Design System squad owns Pathway — the components, tokens, pipeline, and governance. The foundation every other tribe builds on.

The Platform Flows squad owns the cross-module end to end experiences. Profiles, permissions, account settings, sign in. The flows that cut across every module and belong to no single one.

The two squads work as one tribe — shared rituals, shared roadmap, tight coordination.

Plan · 11 of 17
Transition

What happens to the core tribe.

The core tribe and its designer become the Platform Flows squad. This is a move from siloed module work to owning the cross-platform flows that matter most.

The remit shifts toward service design. Designing end to end, with every module in mind, accounting for how flows cross over and impact each other. Everything they design for templated flows, permissions, or settings is built to be reused across the platform and contributed back into Pathway.

Their developers and development managers continue, now working against a shared, governed pipeline rather than a siloed repo. The same people, doing more connected, higher-leverage work.

Plan · 12 of 17
Dependencies & tradeoffs

What this plan depends on.

Engineering
Engineering capacity is the critical dependency. Phase 1 needs one dedicated engineer to ramp the Unity repo to the pipeline and governance model. Phase 2 needs that to scale with front-end support as component build volume grows. Under-resourcing engineering is the single fastest way to stall the plan.
Feature work
This work needs a prioritised reduction in new feature development across the stabilisation period. With more resources, both can run in parallel. The decision is yours.
Rebrand
The launch moment depends on marketing's rebrand readiness. If the rebrand ships earlier, the integrated moment is lost.
Analytics
Reliable baseline data needs dedicated product analytics the organisation isn't set up to deliver for product. A longer term conversation.
Plan · 13 of 17
Risks, named upfront

Named before they surprise us.

Hiring
The lead and backfill designers are critical path. Recruiting starts immediately. Every week this is delayed is a week the timeline slides.
Engineering
The engineering resource not secured in time stalls the plan. Securing it is the first operational decision.
Rebrand timing
If rebrand timing shifts, launch coordination adjusts. Managed through marketing partnership from Phase 1.
Scope creep
Pathway can become a service desk if governance isn't held. The contribution model documented in Phase 1 is the protection.
Component size
Tables, search, and workflow components are large. If they prove larger than estimated, we descope lower-priority components rather than extend the timeline.

One component
One visible component shipped. A high frequency pattern built in the system and rolled out across one or two modules as a pilot.
Measurement
A measurement framework defined. Metrics, methodology, baseline plan.

The floor is real value. The full vision is the ceiling. No version of this leaves us worse off than today.
