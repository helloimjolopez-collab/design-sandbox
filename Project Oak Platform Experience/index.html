Why this matters now

The February rebrand is a market moment. For it to land as a product transformation, the experience underneath the new brand has to hold together.

Right now it does not. The product is fragmented across modules, every team builds independently, patterns are inconsistent, handoffs are heavy and lossy.

Without a shared foundation, the rebrand ships as a coat of paint over a fragmented product. The promise breaks the moment users start clicking and the market moment is wasted.

Project Oak builds that foundation. It is a platform infrastructure investment with a measurable business return.

Product experience fragmentation is costing us in three measurable ways

  1. Support ticket volume
  2. Engineering duplicate, wasted, and rework cost
  3. NRR pressure from customers who adopt fewer modules because the experience does not make expansion feel worth it
"The investment is three permanent roles and a tooling upgrade. The cost of not investing is compounding fragmentation with every sprint."

This is a revenue protection and valuation lever.

Three Tracks

One programme, running in parallel

Three parallel tracks. One programme. Each depends on the others. Track 1 is the engine that makes tracks 2 and 3 possible.

Track 1: Design System Foundation: Pathway
Led by: Experience Design Director, Principal Experience Designer, Principal Design System Engineer
The shared component library, token architecture, and governed pipeline that makes the rebrand deployable at scale and gives every tribe a consistent foundation to build from. This is the engine that powers tracks 2 and 3. Without it, both fail.
Track 2: Rebrand Integration
Led by: Experience Design Director, Principal Experience Designer, Principal Design System Engineer
The new brand visual language applied consistently across every module through the Pathway token architecture. Because the tokens propagate everywhere from a single source, the rebrand is a coordinated deployment, not a six-month module-by-module rollout. Track 1 makes this possible.
Track 3: Component Prioritisation from UI Friction Data
Led by: Experience Design Director
Tribe designers log their top 5 component-level UI friction points per product into the shared friction database. These are interaction-level problems only: inaccessible components, broken states, inconsistent patterns, unusable elements. That data directly shapes the component build queue - the components causing the most pain across tribes get built first. As Pathway components are implemented, those friction points are resolved by definition.

This track is not about flow problems. Flow, IA, service design, and logic or system behaviour issues are out of scope here and belong to a separate programme.

What counts as UI friction for this programme

Not every friction point is the same. The design system squad can only act on one type. Understanding the difference is what makes the friction log useful.

In scope for Project Oak

Problems that exist at the component or interaction level, regardless of which flow they sit in. Fixing the component fixes the problem everywhere it appears.

Examples: A button with no visible focus state. A form field that does not surface validation errors accessibly. A table with no consistent sort or filter behaviour. A side nav that looks and behaves differently in every module. A modal with no keyboard trap. A spinner with no aria-live region. Colour contrast that fails WCAG AA.

The test: if every component on the screen were replaced with a correct Pathway component, would this problem disappear? If yes - it belongs here.

Out of scope for Project Oak

Problems that live in the logic, sequence, architecture, or behaviour of a flow. Fixing the component does not fix these - the product, service design, or engineering model needs to change.

Examples: A success screen shown even when the order failed (system/logic). An email notification sent to users who do not have permission to view the linked content (service design). A sign-in flow that takes too many steps (flow/IA). An error state that does not exist because the product has not defined its error states yet (system, with a future DS dependency).

The test: if every component were perfect, would this problem still exist? If yes - it does not belong in this log.

Three friction types - one tagging model

When logging a friction item, tag it with one of three Scope values:

DS - Component, interaction, pattern, accessibility, or visual consistency issue. Actionable by the design system squad. This is what Project Oak acts on.
Flow - Sequence, IA, navigation, or service design issue. Owned by each tribe individually, with strategic oversight from the Director of Product Design. Not part of Project Oak.
System - Logic, data, state, or backend behaviour issue. Engineering and product own the fix. May create a future DS pattern dependency once the system behaviour is defined, but is not actionable by DS until then.

Flow and experience improvement: a separate programme

Major flow friction points - unclear journeys, broken information architecture, flows that take too long, notification models that do not match user permissions - are real problems and they deserve a real programme. That programme is not Project Oak.

The Experience Director-led UX improvement programme runs in parallel to Project Oak.

Each tribe owns the identification and prioritisation of its high-friction flows. The Director of Product Design provides strategic oversight and leads the cross-tribe view of where the biggest experience gaps are. The design system squad is a dependency of that programme - not its owner. Where a flow improvement requires a component or template that does not yet exist in Pathway, the DS squad prioritises building it. Where a recurring flow pattern is common enough across multiple products, it may become a Pathway template. But the decision to fix a flow, and the work to redesign it, belongs to the tribe and the product design director - not to this programme.

"The Experience Design Director and the principal designer must be confirmed and in place at programme start. With the programme already beginning mid-July, every week these roles remain unconfirmed directly erodes the build phase and threatens the December 1 engineering hard stop."

Figma organisation plan upgrade

Branching, versioning, design system analytics, and full code connect are unavailable on the current plan. Without this, the pipeline cannot function as designed and the rebrand cannot ship consistently. Non-negotiable.

Engineering alignment

Front-end engineers across all tribes need to build from Pathway and use Figma dev mode code connect and the Pathway Code Library as the implementation source of truth. Accurate implementation from the first build reduces rework cycles and sprint cost. This requires a clear mandate from engineering leadership.

Tribe coordinated participation

Designers and PMs across all modules need protected time in the first 30 days to produce the diagnostic and gather the data. This is the planning foundation for the entire programme. Without it, sequencing is guesswork.

How we move at speed

The DS Squad builds against a defined spec and canonical component structure from day one. AI-assisted generation using Radix primitives and Pathway tokens compresses build time significantly. Complex cross-module components - navigation, global search - are owned internally by the DS Squad. Simpler atoms and molecules are built in batches, QA'd against spec, and published to the npm package on a monthly cadence.

How We Measure It

Baselines established in month 1, measured at 6 and 12 months post-launch

The baseline is established in July during the tribe diagnostic. Every metric below is measured against that baseline.

Establishing Pendo baselines in July - who does what

Each tribe's designer + PM pair is responsible for capturing their product's baseline in Pendo before any Pathway implementation begins. The Experience Design Director coordinates timing and ensures all tribes submit by end of July.

What to capture per product:

  • Task completion rate - identify the 3–5 core tasks in the product. If funnel tracking is not already set up in Pendo, set it up now. Capture current completion rate per funnel.
  • Feature and module adoption - export current MAU/DAU and active feature usage rates for the product's primary features.
  • Guides and in-app NPS/CSAT - if Pendo guides are used for survey collection, export current scores and response volumes.
  • Drop-off points - identify the top 3 screens or interactions where users visibly drop off, time out, or require support. Note these by screen name and Pendo path.

Output: a per-product baseline snapshot (Pendo export or screenshot) saved to the Project Oak shared folder by end of July. This is the reference point for all post-launch measurement.

Batch order and rationale

Each batch depends on the previous one. Atoms must exist before molecules can be built. Molecules before organisms. Navigation and organisms can run in parallel because they have different owners within the DS squad and no shared dependencies.

Foundation (July) - Tokens and existing components packaged. No new design. Tribes unblock immediately: apply brand CSS tokens from npm on day 1. Existing components (button, input, checkbox, radio, badge, spinner, side nav, top nav, page shell) are dev reviewed, token wired, and published for import.

Batch 1 - Atoms (August) - Net-new atoms plus a11y + token pass on Foundation atoms. Why first: every molecule, organism, and template composes atoms. Building molecules before atoms are stable creates rework.

Batch 2 - Molecules (September) - Molecules compose atoms. Why second: selects, date pickers, modals, and form groups all use buttons, inputs, checkboxes, and icons internally. The atom layer must be published and stable.

Batch 3 - Organisms (October) - Complex components that compose molecules + atoms. Tables, cards, drawers, and tabs are the highest-frequency, highest-pain surfaces across all modules. Why October: needs the full molecule layer stable. Tables alone represent the single largest cross-module fragmentation point.

Batch 4 - Navigation (October, parallel) - Global navigation shell. Runs in parallel with Batch 3 because navigation is a separate workstream owned by the principal engineer and does not compose organisms. Existing top nav and side nav get token wiring, global search, module switcher, and notification centre added.

Batch 5 - Templates (Post-launch) - Page-level layout templates. Why post-launch: templates compose the entire component library. They cannot be properly specified until the full atom-to-organism stack is stable and tested in production.

Findings

Module Observed violation Cat. Severity
H1  ·  Visibility of system status  ·  Users should always know what is going on via appropriate feedback within reasonable time
Service Planning "My Teams" right panel shows "Loading Teams" with an indefinite spinner on the Schedules page - no timeout, no failure state, no retry affordance ever appears H1 Major
Service Planning Schedules main content area goes completely blank while loading - no skeleton, no loading indicator, no feedback that data is being fetched H1 Major
Giving Admin "Giving Overview" chart section on the dashboard renders as a blank white area - no chart, no empty state message, no loading indicator H1 Major
Cross-module Systemic across all modules: interactive elements produce no loading feedback after click. Button state does not change, no spinner appears, no progress indicator - users receive zero confirmation their action was registered. Observed across Giving Admin, People, Service Planning, and Amplify Home on every form submission, filter application, and navigation action. H1 Critical
Amplify Home Locked sidebar nav items give no feedback on click - no pressed state, no loading indicator, no cursor change. User receives zero acknowledgement that their click registered before being silently transported to an external website in a new tab. H1 Critical
Accounting "Refresh" triggers a full ASP.NET page postback with no loading indicator. The page goes blank during the refresh cycle with no spinner, no progress feedback, and no acknowledgement that the action was received. H1 Major
Media "Top Worship Songs This Week" section renders an empty list with no loading state, no skeleton, and no empty state message - users have no indication whether content is loading or simply absent. H1 Major
H2  ·  Match between system and the real world  ·  Use words, phrases, and concepts familiar to the user, not internal system terminology
Service Planning Page title heading renders "Schedules [No Locations Defined]" - raw system configuration state embedded directly in a UI heading H2 Major
Service Planning Empty state reads "There are no services scheduled in Planning" - "in Planning" references the module product name as a location, ambiguous to users unfamiliar with module naming H2 Minor
Service Planning Profile wizard step 2 is titled "Please update your information" but contains only the text "Introducing...Your Dashboard!!!" - the title directly contradicts the content H2 Minor
Giving Admin "COLLAPSE" rendered as a left nav item alongside navigation links - a UI toggle control presented as if it were a navigation destination H2 Minor
Giving Admin "Env #" field label on donor detail - unexplained abbreviation for "Envelope Number", a church-specific financial concept with no tooltip or glossary H2 Minor
Giving Admin Filter chip shows date range in ISO 8601 format ("2026-06-17 to 2026-07-15") - not a natural date format for US users who expect Jun 17, 2026 H2 Minor
People "ChMS Profiles" tab label - "ChMS" is an internal industry acronym (Church Management Software) not appropriate as end-user UI copy H2 Minor
People "Badge Note" section heading on person profile - church-specific term with no label, tooltip, or explanation for users unfamiliar with the concept H2 Minor
People Search results count reads "1 Results" - grammatically incorrect singular/plural H2 Minor
Accounting Internal build identifier "SFO-QAa3" is displayed in the top header bar alongside the Shelby branding - a raw build reference exposed directly in the production-facing UI H2 Minor
Accounting Navigation section labels (Remittance, Pension Plans, Deposits On Hand, Loan Processing, Insurance Billing, Pension Billing) use accounting-specific financial terminology with no definitions, tooltips, or glossary - inaccessible to users unfamiliar with fund accounting H2 Minor
Settings "My Bundle: No Active Bundle" in Subscription Overview - "Bundle" is platform-internal commercial terminology not defined in context. The same financial settings section is labeled "Billing" in one org and "Invoices" in another org - same concept, two different labels. H2 Major
H3  ·  User control and freedom  ·  Users often choose functions by mistake and need a clearly marked "emergency exit"
Service Planning Profile update wizard fires on every dashboard load and blocks all page content with no dismiss, skip, or close option - user must complete the full 2-step wizard before accessing any functionality H3 Critical
Service Planning Pressing Escape on the wizard navigated the browser to /events/index.cfm which displayed a raw server error ("Missing Template!") - a standard keyboard shortcut triggered unintended destructive navigation with no recovery path H3 Major
People Person detail page has no back button, breadcrumb, or visible navigation path to return to the search results page - the only exit is browser back H3 Major
Amplify Home Locked sidebar nav items (Websites, Streaming) silently open external third-party product sites (ekklesia360.com, churchstreaming.tv) in new browser tabs with zero warning, confirmation dialog, or "opens in new tab" affordance. Users cannot predict what will happen when they click, get no loading feedback, and are unexpectedly removed from the Amplify product context with no path back. Affects all locked/upsell items in the sidebar. H3 Critical
Accounting No way to return to Amplify Home from within Accounting. The module has no Amplify branding, no back link, no module switcher, and no visible connection to the Amplify platform shell. Users who navigate into Accounting are stranded in the legacy app with only the browser back button as an exit. H3 Critical
Settings Delete user (trash icon) is placed directly on every row in the Users admin table with no confirmation dialog. A mis-click or accidental tap permanently removes a user with no undo. Destructive action has no safeguard of any kind. H3 Critical
H4  ·  Consistency and standards  ·  Users should not have to wonder whether different words, situations, or actions mean the same thing
Service Planning Wizard "Next (1/2)" progress button is positioned in the top-right corner of the viewport - standard form progression controls belong inline at the bottom of the form H4 Minor
People Two simultaneous search inputs visible at the same time: global search bar in the top nav ("Search for a person by name") and a second in-page search field - both serving the same lookup function H4 Major
Cross-module Global top nav composition is completely different across every module - Giving Admin has no search bar; People has a prominent global search plus filter icon; Service Planning has a minimal nav with no search. Users cannot build a consistent mental model of the navigation shell. This is a systemic architectural inconsistency, not a per-module quirk. H4 Critical
Accounting Accounting is an entirely different application (Shelby Systems legacy ASP.NET) with its own nav paradigm - a top-bar module menu (15+ accounting sections), a collapsed left sidebar (Security Setup, Utilities, Help), no Amplify shell, different branding, and a different interaction model throughout. Users experience a complete context break when entering Accounting from any other module. H4 Critical
Media Media uses a third distinct navigation paradigm: icon-only left sidebar (no text labels) and a horizontal category nav bar above content - different from the Amplify Home sidebar, different from Accounting's top menu bar, different from People's hybrid nav. H4 Critical
Cross-module Sidebar module ordering differs across organizations. Switching orgs reorders all modules in the sidebar, disorienting users who work across multiple organizations and rely on muscle memory for module location. H4 Major
Settings Settings sub-section labels differ between organizations: "Billing" in renmore_test1 vs "Invoices" in Safety Module Test org - the same financial section carries different labels depending on org configuration. H4 Major
Accounting Page title in browser tab reads "Financials Home" but the navigation button and module name reads "Accounting Home" - two different names for the same landing page within the same module. H4 Minor
H5  ·  Error prevention  ·  Carefully designed systems prevent problems from occurring in the first place
Service Planning On the Schedules page, an inline empty state and an unsolicited guidance modal appear simultaneously, each suggesting a different next step ("Click here to create one" vs "Go to Teams") - conflicting guidance fires before any user action H5 Major
Service Planning The empty state tells users to "Please adjust the location filter above" - no location filter is visible on screen; the real problem is a system configuration gap, not a user filter selection error H5 Major
People "Order Background Check" button is prominently surfaced on every person detail view regardless of user role or workflow context - a sensitive, consequential action with no contextual gate H5 Minor
H6  ·  Recognition rather than recall  ·  Minimize the user's memory load by making objects, actions, and options visible
People Five action buttons on the person detail page top-right are unlabeled icons - function is not discoverable at default state and requires hover to reveal tooltips (share, document, chat, download, edit) H6 Major
Media Media sidebar is icon-only with no text labels - all navigation is hidden behind icon recognition with no fallback. There is no hover tooltip and no expanded/text mode, so function is entirely dependent on user learning the icon vocabulary. H6 Critical
Settings Subscription Overview section displays rows of clock/timer icons with no label, tooltip, or context - their meaning is not stated anywhere on the page. H6 Major
Amplify Home Org switcher module icons are small colored circles with no text labels - a user who has not memorized which circle maps to which module has no recovery path. H6 Major
H7  ·  Flexibility and efficiency of use  ·  Accelerators - unseen by novice users - may speed up interaction for expert users
Giving Admin Donor list table column headers are not sortable - no click affordance or sort direction indicator H7 Minor
Cross-module No keyboard shortcuts, command palette, or bulk selection actions observed in any of the three assessed modules H7 Minor

Service Planning Profile wizard step 2 contains only the heading "Introducing...Your Dashboard!!!" with three exclamation marks and no other content - decorative text with zero informational value occupies a full modal step H8 Minor
Service Planning Inline empty state is rendered behind a simultaneously triggered guidance modal on the Schedules page - two competing feedback mechanisms on the same screen at the same time H8 Major
Giving Admin Address column in the donor list table is almost entirely empty across all visible rows, consuming approximately 15% of table width with near-zero data density H8 Minor
Giving Admin Dashboard displays three separate stat card sections (top header row, "All Time", "Last 30 Days") using similar but not identical card patterns - increased cognitive load without proportional clarity gain H8 Minor
People Person profile Details section shows field label stubs with empty values - Address renders as "," (bare comma), Location and Last Login show as label-only rows - ghost fields increase vertical noise H8 Minor

Service Planning "Before you schedule anyone to serve..." guidance modal provides useful onboarding context but fires unsolicited on every navigation to the Schedules area with no "don't show again" option - guidance functions as repeated friction rather than targeted help H10 Minor

Church staff without accounting training have no in-product path to understanding these terms. H10 Major
Accessibility  ·  WCAG 2.1 AA  ·  Full automated audit (axe-core 4.9.1) + manual DOM inspection on Amplify Home and Auth0 login. Directly observed violations in People and Service Planning.
Platform-wide CSS outline: none is applied globally to all interactive elements, removing all keyboard focus indicators across the entire platform. Keyboard users cannot see which element has focus at any time. Fails WCAG 2.4.7 Focus Visible (AA). Affects every module. A11y Critical
Platform-wide No skip navigation link exists anywhere in the platform. Keyboard users must tab through all sidebar navigation items (14+) on every page load before reaching main content. Fails WCAG 2.4.1 Bypass Blocks (AA). A11y Critical
Amplify Home No <main> landmark exists anywhere in the application shell. All page content sits outside landmark regions, making it impossible for screen reader users to navigate directly to the main content area. Fails WCAG 1.3.6 Identify Purpose. A11y Critical
Amplify Home Sidebar navigation is not wrapped in a <nav> landmark element. Screen reader users cannot jump directly to navigation using landmark shortcuts. Fails WCAG 1.3.6. A11y Major
Amplify Home Heading hierarchy is broken: DOM order is H2 > H2 > H1 > H4 > H4 > H3. The page's first H1 is the third heading encountered; heading levels skip from H1 directly to H4 with no H2 or H3 in between. Screen reader users relying on heading navigation encounter an incoherent document structure. Fails WCAG 1.3.1 Info and Relationships. A11y Major
Amplify Home Two role="progressbar" elements (profile completion indicators) are missing all required ARIA attributes: aria-label, aria-valuenow, and aria-valuemax. Screen readers cannot announce their current value or purpose. Fails WCAG 4.1.2 Name, Role, Value. A11y Major
Amplify Home Active sidebar nav item has no aria-current="page" attribute. Screen reader users cannot determine which section is currently active without visually scanning the highlighted item. Fails WCAG 4.1.2. A11y Major
Amplify Home Locked nav item decorative lock icons carry alt="Click to learn more or upgrade". This alt text is concatenated into the button's accessible name, producing misleading announcements such as "Websites Click to learn more or upgrade" for screen reader users. A11y Minor
Auth0 Login Login page has no <main> landmark, no H1 heading, and 7 content nodes sitting outside any landmark region. Screen readers have no structural entry point into the page. Fails WCAG 1.3.6 and 1.3.1. A11y Major
People Five icon-only action buttons on the person detail page (share, document, chat, download, edit) have no visible text labels. Accessible names cannot be determined without hovering. Fails WCAG 4.1.2 Name, Role, Value. A11y Major
Service Planning Profile setup wizard fires on dashboard load, blocks all content, and cannot be closed via keyboard without triggering unintended browser navigation (Escape navigates to /events/index.cfm). Keyboard-dependent users are fully trapped. Fails WCAG 2.1.2 No Keyboard Trap. A11y Critical
Accounting Multiple navigation links throughout Accounting use href="javascript:__doPostBack(...)" and href="javascript:WebForm_DoPostBackWithOptions(...)". These are functionally buttons implemented as links - incorrect semantics confuse screen readers and break keyboard interaction expectations. Fails WCAG 4.1.2 Name, Role, Value. A11y Major
Accounting Date pickers throughout Accounting use type="text" inputs with no aria-describedby or visible format hint (MM/DD/YYYY). The calendar popup has no aria-label, no role="dialog", and no aria-modal attribute. Screen reader users have no way to determine the expected input format or navigate the popup accessibly. Fails WCAG 1.3.1 and 4.1.2. A11y Major
Accounting The entire Accounting page content is wrapped in a single <form> element, a legacy ASP.NET WebForms pattern. This exposes all interactive elements - including navigation links - to accidental form submission semantics and prevents correct landmark-based navigation. Fails WCAG 1.3.6. A11y Major
Media Each content card in the Media browse view contains 4 separate links to the same destination (card title link, thumbnail link, bookmark link, and a detail link). Duplicate links multiply the number of tab stops without adding navigational value, significantly increasing cognitive load for keyboard and screen reader users. Fails WCAG 2.4.4 Link Purpose. A11y Major
Media Bookmark <a> elements are nested inside the card's outer <a> link - invalid HTML per spec (interactive element inside an interactive element). Browsers handle this inconsistently and screen readers may skip the inner link entirely. Fails WCAG 4.1.1 Parsing. A11y Major
