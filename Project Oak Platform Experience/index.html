Why this matters now

The February rebrand is a market moment. For it to land as a product transformation, the experience underneath the new brand has to hold together.

Right now it does not. The product is fragmented across modules, every team builds independently, patterns are inconsistent, handoffs are heavy and lossy.

Without a shared foundation, the rebrand ships as a coat of paint over a fragmented product. The promise breaks the moment users start clicking and the market moment is wasted.

Project Oak builds that foundation. It is a platform infrastructure investment with a measurable business return.

Product experience fragmentation is costing us in three measurable ways

  1. Support ticket volume
  2. Engineering duplicate, wasted, and rework cost
  3. NRR pressure from customers who adopt fewer modules because the experience does not make expansion feel worth it
"The investment is three permanent roles, a tooling upgrade, and activation of existing Value Labs resource. The cost of not investing is compounding fragmentation with every sprint."

This is a revenue protection and valuation lever.

Three Tracks

One programme, running in parallel

Three parallel tracks. One programme. Each depends on the others. Track 1 is the engine that makes tracks 2 and 3 possible.

Track 1: Design System Foundation: Pathway
Led by: Experience Design Director, Principal Experience Designer, Principal Design System Engineer
The shared component library, token architecture, and governed pipeline that makes the rebrand deployable at scale and gives every tribe a consistent foundation to build from. This is the engine that powers tracks 2 and 3. Without it, both fail.
Track 2: Rebrand Integration
Led by: Experience Design Director, Principal Experience Designer, Principal Design System Engineer
The new brand visual language applied consistently across every module through the Pathway token architecture. Because the tokens propagate everywhere from a single source, the rebrand is a coordinated deployment, not a six-month module-by-module rollout. Track 1 makes this possible.
Track 3: Highest Friction UX Improvements
Led by: Experience Design Director, Director of Product Management
Every product tribe works in parallel with the design system team to identify, prioritise, and tackle the most pressing usability and experience friction points across their flows. The components tribes need most get built first. The result is a product that not only looks different in February but works meaningfully better.

  • Full WCAG 2.1 AA compliance across the entire product (full compliance only on components delivered by February)

Execution Plan

First 30 days, then month by month through February 2027

The First 30 Days

This is the planning foundation for the entire programme. Everything in the month-by-month plan below is sequenced on what comes out of this phase. Two steps, running in parallel with baseline metrics work.

01
Tribe diagnostic
Led by: Experience Design Director, Director of Product Management

Every designer and PM across all modules produces a short structured report covering:

  1. The top three core flows in their product with the highest user friction.
  2. Their top five UX debt items ranked by impact category and priority.
  3. Current data gathered with product ops and the Pendo team: NPS, Time on task, Task Completion Rate, Drop Off Rate, Sentiment metrics across core flows.
"The output gives the design system team a prioritised map of what components and patterns to build first, and gives each tribe a clear brief for their experience improvement work."

This data serves two purposes. First, it informs sequencing: with this data in hand, the design system team can optimise component delivery to support tribes in tackling their highest friction, highest traffic, highest impact areas. Second, it establishes the baseline against which all metrics are measured after the February launch. Right now this data is fragmented and inconsistently instrumented across modules. Getting it into a reliable, shared state is a prerequisite for making smart decisions about where to invest.

02
Sequencing the build
Led by: Experience Design Director

The outputs from Step 1 feed directly into the design system component build plan and the tribe experience roadmaps. The components tribes need most get built first. The flows with the highest friction get addressed first. Everything is sequenced around maximum impact by February.

~
In parallel: Design System Baseline Metrics Benchmarking
Led by: Principal Experience Designer, Principal Design System Engineer

Running concurrently with Steps 1 and 2, the design system team establishes baseline performance metrics for the Pathway system itself. This gives the programme a clear before/after picture on platform efficiency and adoption.

What's Required

To succeed and scale at speed

Design System Squad: Three permanent roles

1
Experience Design Director
Owning the experience vision, prioritising and partnering strategically with product leadership.
2
Principal-Level Experience Designer
Owning the design system authoring, governance and contribution model, direction, and quality bar. Permanent platform infrastructure ownership, not a project role.
3
Principal-Level Design Systems Engineer
Owning the technical pipeline, governance, token architecture, Storybook, and code connect. Also permanent. The system drifts immediately without continuous engineering ownership.
"Pathway is a platform layer. It requires permanent ownership. The next seven months deliver the February launch. The permanent roles ensure the platform continues to compound beyond it."
"The Experience Design Director and the principal designer need to be confirmed and in place by June. If either role slips beyond June, the programme loses its two most critical planning and leadership months and the December 1st engineering hard stop becomes very difficult to meet."

Figma organisation plan upgrade

Branching, versioning, design system analytics, and full code connect are unavailable on the current plan. Without this, the pipeline cannot function as designed and the rebrand cannot ship consistently. Non-negotiable.

Engineering alignment

Front-end engineers across all tribes need to build from Pathway and use Figma dev mode code connect and the Pathway Code Library as the implementation source of truth. Accurate implementation from the first build reduces rework cycles and sprint cost. This requires a clear mandate from engineering leadership.

Tribe coordinated participation

Designers and PMs across all modules need protected time in the first 30 days to produce the diagnostic and gather the data. This is the planning foundation for the entire programme. Without it, sequencing is guesswork.

How we move at speed

We do not need to wait for new hires to start shipping. The programme activates immediately on existing resources.

Value Labs are already engaged on Protections for component delivery. We activate them now as a dedicated component delivery resource under design leadership. The complete spec canonical structure and component build guidelines and handover requirements are defined: Value Labs design and document against that spec. Components are reviewed and pushed through the Pathway pipeline.

Value Labs start on: tables, table controls, dashboard KPI tiles, filter chips, search and select, advanced search, steppers and progress indicators, update of existing Input and Select components, cards.

Internally, AI-assisted generation using Radix primitives and Pathway tokens further compresses the build timeline for components built by the Design System Team. Complex cross-module components (navigation, global search) stay internal. Everything else accelerates through Value Labs.
